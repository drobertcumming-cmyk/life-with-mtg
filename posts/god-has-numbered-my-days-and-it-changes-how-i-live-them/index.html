God Has Numbered My Days — and It Changes How I Live Them

By Marjorie Taylor Greene

May 21, 2026 · 3 min read

I'm a Christian, and I believe that God has numbered my days.

I've said that publicly, and I meant it in a specific way I want to explain, because it shapes how I live more than almost anything else I could tell you.

I'm a Christian, and I believe that God has numbered my days.

I take care of my health. I've been open about the treatments I've sought and the choices I've made to stay strong and well. But I don't do any of it believing I'll add a single day to my life beyond what God has already set for me. I don't think that's how it works. I believe He has numbered my days, every one of them, and that isn't in my hands.

So what's the point of taking care of yourself, if the number's already set? Here's how I hold it. I can't change how many days I'm given. But I can do something about the quality of those days. I can be strong instead of frail, clear instead of foggy, present instead of worn out — for the people I love and the work I'm called to do. Stewardship of the body God gave me, for the time He's given me. Those two beliefs don't fight each other. They fit together.

There's a freedom in this that I wish more people had. When you truly believe your days are numbered by God, you stop living in fear of death — and once you're not afraid of that, there's very little left that can control you. It's a big part of why I can say what I say and take the hits I take. I'm not fearless because I'm tough. I'm settled because I know Who holds my days.

It also changes how I spend those days. If they're numbered and I don't know the number, then none of them are throwaways. That makes you less patient with things that don't matter and more devoted to the things that do — your faith, your family, your purpose. It puts a kind of weight on ordinary time that's easy to miss when you assume you've got forever.

I don't preach at people, and I'm not going to start now. Faith is personal, and yours is yours. But you asked to know me, and this is the center of me — not a talking point, the actual foundation I stand on when everything else is shaking.

God has numbered my days. I intend to live every one of them well, use them for something that matters, and not waste a single one being afraid. I'd wish the same for you.
