The Gold Scam That Targets the People Who Trust the Most

By Marjorie Taylor Greene

Jun 6, 2026 · 3 min read

I built my name on following the money and calling out people who prey on the trusting. So I want to warn you about a scam that targets the exact people I care about most — retirees and conservatives trying to protect their savings by owning something real.

The instinct is a good one. When you don't trust the dollar and you don't trust Washington, wanting to own real, physical gold makes complete sense. The problem isn't the instinct. It's that a slice of the precious-metals industry has learned to exploit it.

Here's the play, and I want you to see it clearly, because it depends on you not seeing it.

You call about gold. Instead of selling you plain, standard bullion at a fair price, a salesman steers you toward "exclusive" or "collector" coins — what the industry calls numismatics. They'll tell you these are special, rare, a better deal. Here's what they don't tell you. Those coins can carry enormous markups baked into the price, sometimes far above what the metal is actually worth. And when you go to sell, there's often no real market for them, so you can't get your money back out at anything close to what you paid.

Then there's the spread — the gap between the price they sell to you at and the price they'll buy back from you at. In the worst cases that gap is enormous, and it's buried where you'll never see it until it's too late. You hand over your retirement savings, and a huge chunk of it disappears into someone else's margin before you've even started.

This makes me furious, because the people getting hurt are the people who did the right thing. They saw the problem. They took action. And they got taken advantage of by an industry that wraps itself in patriotism and faith while picking their pockets.

So here's what I want you to know. If someone is pushing you toward "exclusive" coins, "rare" coins, or anything that isn't plain, standard bullion — ask why. Ask what the markup is. Ask what they'll buy it back from you at tomorrow. And if they can't give you a straight answer, walk away.

Do your own research. Understand the difference between bullion and numismatics. Look at what a fair spread actually looks like. Because the people who prey on trust are counting on you not asking those questions — and the best defense you have is knowing exactly what to ask.
